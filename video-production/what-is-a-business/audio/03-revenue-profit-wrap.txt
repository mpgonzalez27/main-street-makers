[warm][encouraging] At book club, five children buy bookmarks.

Nora earns five coins.

Money that comes into the business is called revenue.

But revenue is not the same as profit.

Profit is what is left after expenses.

Nora earned five coins. She spent four coins on supplies.

Five coins in minus four coins out leaves one coin.

That one coin is profit.

Now Nora can make a wise choice.

She could save the coin.

She could buy more ribbon.

She could make better signs.

She could ask customers what colors they like best.

That is market research. Market research means learning what customers need before you make more.

The SBA teaches grown-up business owners to think about customers, costs, and how a business will make money. We can learn the same idea in a kid-sized way.

So, what is a business?

A business is not just a money machine.

A business is a planned way to help people.

It notices a need.

It makes an offer.

It serves customers.

It keeps records.

It learns from what happens.

And step by step, it asks, “Is this helping people? Is this working? What should I do next?”
